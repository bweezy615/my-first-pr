# Raffles in Tennessee

## Can You Hold a Raffle in Tennessee?

**Yes — but only qualifying nonprofit organizations can legally hold raffles in Tennessee.**

Tennessee law (T.C.A. § 39-17-507) exempts certain charitable raffles from gambling statutes, with specific requirements.

## Who Can Run a Raffle

Only qualified nonprofit organizations, including:

- Nonprofit charitable organizations (501(c)(3))
- Nonprofit civic organizations (Rotary, Kiwanis, Lions, etc.)
- Nonprofit educational organizations
- Nonprofit historical organizations
- Nonprofit fraternal organizations
- Religious organizations

For-profit businesses and individuals **cannot** legally hold raffles in Tennessee.

## Key Rules

| Requirement | Detail |
|---|---|
| Organization type | Must be a qualifying nonprofit |
| Purpose | Proceeds must benefit the nonprofit's charitable mission |
| Prize limits | No statutory cap, but check local ordinances |
| Ticket sales | Must be conducted by members of the organization |
| Paid staff | Raffle workers generally must be volunteers, not paid employees |
| Reporting | May need to report proceeds depending on amount |

## What Counts as a Raffle

A raffle requires three elements:
1. **Prize** — something of value awarded to a winner
2. **Chance** — winner selected randomly
3. **Consideration** — participants pay or provide something of value for a ticket

If any element is missing, it may not be a raffle under the law (e.g., free entry removes "consideration").

## Local Permits

Some Tennessee cities and counties require a permit or license before holding a raffle. Always check with your **local city or county clerk** before organizing one.

## Penalties for Illegal Raffles

Running an unlawful lottery or raffle in Tennessee is a **Class C misdemeanor**, which can result in fines up to $500.
